# OSLRS Enumerator Field Guide

Read this once before your first day. Keep it on your phone.

Version 2026-09-29 · Oyo State Labour & Skills Registry

You can download this guide, and your ID card, from your dashboard after you log in.

---

## Part 1 — Getting started

### 1.1 The invitation email

You will receive an email inviting you to activate your OSLRS enumerator account. Open it on the
phone you will actually work with. The link is personal to you — do not forward it to anyone.

If it has not arrived, check your spam folder first, then tell your supervisor. Do not create an
account yourself; enumerator accounts are issued by the office.

### 1.2 Activation — five steps, once

The link opens a short wizard. You cannot skip steps, and you only do this once.

- Set your password
- Your personal information
- Your bank details (this is how you are paid — check the account number twice)
- Your next of kin
- Your selfie

> ### THE SELFIE IS NOT OPTIONAL, AND IT BECOMES YOUR ID CARD
>
> The photo you take at activation is printed on your OSLRS identity card. Take it in good light,
> facing the camera, with your face clear and unobstructed. If there is no photo on your account,
> no ID card can be produced for you — and the card is what tells a stranger at their door who you
> are.

### 1.3 Your ID card and this guide

Once you have logged in, your dashboard offers two downloads:

- Your OSLRS identity card
- This field guide

Download both before you go out. Save the card to your phone's gallery and keep this guide where
you can open it without signal. Both are generated fresh each time you download them, so if
anything changes you simply download again.

### 1.4 Practise before you meet anyone

Do one complete practice survey before your first real interview, and use an obviously fake name
for it. Never practise on a real person's details.

> A practice entry made with a real person's NIN will block that person from registering later,
> because the register refuses a NIN that is already used. Tell your supervisor about every
> practice entry so it can be removed.

## Part 2 — What the survey actually is

The form has eight sections and 47 questions in total, but nobody answers 47. Most people answer
between 25 and 35. The form hides what does not apply to the person in front of you.

The eight sections, in order:

- General — the location, taken automatically
- Introduction and consent
- Identity and demographics
- Labour force participation
- Parent or guardian consent
- Household and welfare
- Skills and business
- Public skills marketplace

One question is shown per screen. Answer it, press Continue, and the form works out what to ask
next. Use Back if you need to change an earlier answer.

## Part 3 — Why questions appear and disappear

This is the part worth understanding properly. The form is not a fixed list; earlier answers open
and close later questions. If you enter something wrong and then correct it, the questions change
underneath you — that is the form working, not a fault.

### 3.1 Consent decides whether there is a survey at all

The second question asks whether the person consents to take part.

> If they say No, stop. Use "Discard this interview" at the bottom of the form. Do not continue,
> and do not submit anything. A person who has not consented must not be in the register.

### 3.2 The date of birth silently decides the whole middle of the form

The form works out the person's age from their date of birth, and that age chooses between two
entirely different sections:

- Aged 15 or over — they get the Labour Force Participation section, about work, occupation,
  hours and income.
- Under 15 — they get the Parent or Guardian Consent section instead, and are never asked about
  employment.

> Get the date of birth right. A wrong year sends the interview down the wrong path without
> warning you. If you find yourself being asked for a parent's details for a grown adult, or being
> asked about someone's salary when they are a child, go Back and check the date of birth first.

For anyone under 15, a parent or guardian must be present and must consent. You will be asked for
the guardian's name, their relationship to the child, their phone number, and their consent. You
are also asked whether the young person is a supervised apprentice, and if so, for a brief
description of the trade.

### 3.3 Work questions follow the first work answer

The first labour question asks whether they worked for pay or profit in the last seven days.

- Yes — you are asked their main occupation, type of employment, years of experience, hours worked
  last week, and estimated monthly income.
- No — you are asked whether they were temporarily absent from a job. If yes, you still get the
  occupation questions. If no, you are asked whether they looked for work in the last four weeks,
  and if not, whether they are available to start within two weeks.

That chain is how unemployment is measured properly. Someone who is not working, not looking, and
not available is counted differently from someone who is actively seeking work — so answer each
one as asked rather than guessing ahead.

### 3.4 Business questions only open if they have a business

Answer Yes to owning or operating a business and you are asked for the business name, whether it
is registered with CAC, the premises address, and the number of apprentices. Answer No and none of
those appear.

### 3.5 The marketplace is opt-in, twice

The last section asks whether they want to join the Skills Marketplace, which connects workers
with employers.

- Say No and the section ends there.
- Say Yes and they are asked a second question: whether employers may see their name and phone
  number. Only if they agree to that are they asked for a short bio and a portfolio link.

Both are genuine choices. Someone can decline the marketplace entirely and still be fully
registered — say so plainly, because people worry that refusing costs them their registration.

## Part 4 — Location

The app captures the location by itself the moment you open a survey. You do not have to press
anything. Carry on answering questions while it works — nothing waits for it.

Open the survey at the door, where the interview happens. Not later in the car, not back at the
office. That is the moment the location is taken, and the point is to record where the work was
actually done.

The first time, your phone asks "Allow location?". Choose Allow.

> If that box appears and you tap it away without answering, the app is left believing you refused.
> Answer it. It is two seconds, and it is the most common reason a survey ends up with no location
> on it.

Your phone also has its own location switch, separate from the app's permission. Swipe down from
the top of the screen and check the Location icon is on. Allowing the app is not enough if the
phone's own location is switched off — and nothing prompts you about that one.

If no location arrives, the app tells you what to do about it and gives you a button to try again.
Read what it says: it is different depending on what your phone reported. Sometimes the answer is
to switch Location on, sometimes it is to step outside or near a window, and sometimes it is to
allow the site again.

If you reach the end and the survey will not submit because there is no location:

- The amber panel now has its own Capture GPS Location button. Fix whatever it tells you to fix,
  press that button, and the survey submits by itself. You do not have to go back through the form.
- If your phone genuinely will not give a location, the panel offers "I could not capture a
  location". Press it and the survey goes through. The app records the reason your phone gave.
- On some failures that button appears only after you have tried once. That is deliberate — most
  of those failures clear on a second attempt.

> Never abandon an interview over a location. A survey recorded with a reason is a real
> registration. Tell your supervisor the same day and say where you were.

Why this matters: the location is how the office knows which areas have been covered, and it is
what protects you — it is the record that you were there.

This does mean the app knows where you are while you are working. It records the location of each
interview, at the interview. It is not tracking you between them, and it is not running when you
are not in a survey.

## Part 5 — What goes in each box

### Names

Surname and first name are required. Enter them as the person says them, in normal capitals — not
all capitals.

### Phone number

Eleven digits, starting with zero. Nigerian mobile numbers only.

- Correct: 08031234567, 07011234567, 09091234567
- Wrong: +2348031234567, 234 803 123 4567, 0803 123 4567

No spaces, no dashes, no country code. The same rule applies to a guardian's phone number.

### Email

Optional. If they give one, they receive their registration number by email automatically. If they
do not, you are the only way they will ever learn their number.

### NIN

Exactly eleven digits, numbers only.

> The app checks that a NIN is eleven digits. It cannot check that it is a real NIN. An invented
> eleven-digit number will be accepted and will sit in the register looking correct. Read it back
> to the person from their card, digit by digit.

If they do not have it with them, use the "I don't have my NIN now" link under the question. You
may add a short reason. The person can supply it later — the system emails them a link. Never turn
someone away for not having their NIN.

### Date of birth

Use the date picker. See section 3.2 — this answer changes the form.

### Choices with fixed answers

- Gender: Male, Female, Prefer not to say
- Marital status: Single, Married, Divorced, Widowed, Separated
- Highest education completed: No Formal Education, Primary School, Junior Secondary (JSS), Senior
  Secondary (SSS/WAEC), Vocational/Technical Training, NCE/OND, HND/Bachelor's Degree, Master's
  Degree, Doctorate/PhD
- Local Government Area: all 33 Oyo State LGAs
- Type of employment: Wage Earner (Government/Public Sector), Wage Earner (Private Sector),
  Self-Employed (Artisan/Trader/Business Owner), Contractor/Consultant, Unpaid Family Worker,
  Apprentice/Intern
- Years of experience: Less than 1 year, 1-3 years, 4-6 years, 7-10 years, Over 10 years
- Housing: Owned, Rented, Living with Family (Free), Employer-Provided, Other
- Business registered with CAC: Yes registered, No, Registration in progress
- Relationship to the young person: Parent, Legal Guardian, Other (relative or caregiver)

> Years of experience stops at "Over 10 years". Someone with thirty years' experience is recorded
> the same as someone with eleven. That is the highest band there is — do not go looking for
> another one.

### Numbers

- Household size: a whole number, at least 1
- Dependents: a whole number, and it should not be more than the household size. The app will not
  stop you if it is, so check it yourself.
- Hours worked last week: between 0 and 168
- Estimated monthly income: optional, in Naira, digits only, 0 or more
- Number of apprentices: optional, 0 or more

### Free text with limits

- Main occupation: what they actually do — tailor, farmer, trader, mechanic
- Other skills not listed: up to 200 characters
- Apprenticeship description: up to 200 characters
- Professional bio: up to 150 characters

### Skills

Primary skills is required and lets you pick several. There are 192 skills in the list — use the
search box rather than scrolling. If what they do is genuinely not there, pick the closest and add
the exact trade under "Other skills not listed above".

Skills they would like to learn is optional and also allows several.

## Part 6 — The registration number

Every person you register gets an OSLRS number that looks like OSL-2026-ABC123. It is how they
prove they are on the register.

> ### DO NOT READ OUT A NUMBER UNTIL THE APP SHOWS YOU ONE
>
> When you finish a survey, the completion screen shows the number only once it has uploaded. If it
> says "Not issued yet — this entry has not finished uploading", then no number exists yet. Do not
> invent one, and do not read one from an earlier screen.
>
> Telling someone a number that does not exist is worse than telling them nothing. They will only
> find out when they try to use it, months later, and they will have no way to prove what you said.

What to say instead:

> "Your registration is saved. Your number is being issued now — I will give it to you before I
> leave, or I will send it to you today."

Then get the number from Sync Status.

## Part 7 — Sync Status, where the numbers actually live

Open Sync Status from the enumerator menu. Every survey you have taken is listed with the
respondent's name and either their OSL-2026 number, or "No number yet — not uploaded".

Make this a habit: once you have signal, open Sync Status and write each number onto that person's
slip. Nothing reminds you to do this. It is on you.

### Households sharing one phone

Several people in one compound often share a handset. That is normal and each person still gets
their own record. But it has one consequence:

> A person on a shared phone cannot look themselves up by phone number later. The system refuses to
> guess which of them is which — correctly, because guessing would show one person another person's
> registration. For anyone on a shared handset, their OSLRS number is the only way back to their
> record.

## Part 8 — When something goes wrong

### Discarding an interview

Use "Discard this interview" at the bottom of the form if the respondent changes their mind, if it
is the wrong person, or if they decline consent.

- It works at any point in the form
- It deletes everything entered, and cannot be undone
- It submits nothing, and no number is issued

Do not leave a half-finished form sitting on the device. The next respondent needs a clean start.

### A survey that failed to upload

Open Sync Status. If an entry is marked rejected:

- Reopen puts it back in your drafts so you can fix what is missing and send it again. Try this
  first — the answers are kept.
- Discard deletes it permanently, and the person would have to be interviewed again from scratch.

Always try Reopen before Discard.

## Part 9 — Questions people ask

"Do I need my NIN?" No. They can register without it and add it later. Never turn someone away.

"Is my information safe?" Yes. It is held by the Oyo State registry. They choose whether to appear
in the Skills Marketplace, and they can decline and still be registered.

"Can I change my answers later?" Yes, using their OSLRS number at oyoskills.com, under check
registration.

"Do I have to join the marketplace?" No, and it costs them nothing to refuse.

"Why do you need my date of birth?" Because the questions that follow depend on age, and because
the register reports on youth employment.

## Part 10 — Before you go out

- Phone charged, and a way to charge it in the field
- Logged in, and the app opens without asking you to log in again
- Location permission set to Allow, and your phone's own Location switch on
- One practice survey completed, with a fake name, and you have seen where the number appears
- Your ID card downloaded and on your phone
- This guide downloaded
- Slips or a notebook for writing numbers onto
- You know how to reach your supervisor

## For supervisors

Never use real people's details to test. Use obviously fake names and phone numbers, and tell the
office so the rows can be removed. Test entries left in the register become real-looking records
that nobody can tell apart from citizens.

Watch for: enumerators reading out numbers before they exist (Part 6); surveys arriving with no
location (Part 4 — ask where they were and whether the phone refused); entries sitting unsynced for
days, which are people who have not been told their number; and dependents recorded as greater than
household size, which the app does not currently reject.
